When is a DPIA required? Article 35(3) checklist

The three cases in which Article 35(3) says a data protection impact assessment is required in particular; each country’s list adds more. 3 points, each the GDPR’s own words with one plain line to tick. Free; your ticks stay in this browser.

When is a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) required?

The general rule is Article 35(1): a DPIA is required where processing "is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons". Article 35(3) names three cases in particular: automated evaluation, including profiling, with legal or similarly significant effects; large-scale processing of special categories or criminal-offence data; large-scale systematic monitoring of a public area.

Source: Article 35 GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679), as held on Fino. Not legal advice.

The checklist

None ticked yet
  1. Article 35(3)(a): the law’s words
    “a systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects relating to natural persons which is based on automated processing, including profiling, and on which decisions are based that produce legal effects concerning the natural person or similarly significantly affect the natural person;”
  2. Article 35(3)(b): the law’s words
    “processing on a large scale of special categories of data referred to in Article 9(1), or of personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences referred to in Article 10; or”
  3. Article 35(3)(c): the law’s words
    “a systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale.”

Tick any that apply: for each one, Article 35(3) says an assessment is required, unless Article 35(10) applies (processing for a legal obligation or a public task, under an EU or national law that regulates it and whose adoption already included an impact assessment, unless the country requires one anyway). Each country’s authority also publishes its own list of processing that needs one (Article 35(4)).

This list covers what the GDPR itself requires. Your national law and your regulator’s guidance can add more. A checklist does not replace reading the article. Not legal advice.
