Documents/Reply to a data request: the answer

Documents · Letter · EU, Italy, Germany

Reply to a data request: the answer

Two replies: the answer to an access request, item by item as Article 15 lists them, and the confirmation that data has been corrected, erased, restricted, transferred or no longer used, with the notice to recipients.

Also in: Italiano · Deutsch

For

Organisations in the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway that receive a request under Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR (the EU General Data Protection Regulation): the data protection officer, privacy team or small-business owner who answers. Notes for Italy and Germany where the rules differ.

Not covered

Formats and procedures set by sector law (for example medical records); the portability format for a given industry; national rules on which authority to name, beyond Italy and Germany (see the person letters in this set).

Law as of

GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) as published on EUR-Lex, read 7 Nov 2026; EDPB Guidelines 01/2022 on the right of access, version 2.1 (adopted 28 March 2023, minor corrections 30 May 2024); Regulation (EEC, Euratom) 1182/71, Article 3; Italian Data Protection Code (Legislative Decree 196/2003) as on Normattiva (updated to the act published on 19 Feb 2026), read 4 to 8 Nov 2026; Garante complaint page (docweb 4535524), read 7 Nov 2026; BDSG as published on gesetze-im-internet.de, last amended by the Act of 12 May 2026, read 7 Nov 2026

01

Answering an access request

The European Data Protection Board (EDPB), the body of all EU data protection regulators, describes three parts: confirmation, access to the data (mainly by a copy), and the information in Article 15(1) and (2) (EDPB Guidelines 01/2022, pp. 3 to 4). The information may start from the privacy notice or the record of processing, "but may have to be updated and tailored" to the person (para. 20, p. 13).

ItemWhat to writeWhere
ConfirmationWhether you process data about the person. If not, say so.Article 15(1)
Copy of the dataAll the data, in a form they can keep. If they asked by e-mail, "in a commonly used electronic form". First copy free.Article 15(3)
PurposesThe purposes in this person's case, and which data serves which purpose; the EDPB recommends adding the legal basis15(1)(a); EDPB para. 114, p. 38
CategoriesOnly the categories that apply to this person15(1)(b); EDPB para. 115, p. 38
RecipientsThe actual recipients by name, unless impossible or the request is manifestly unfounded or excessive15(1)(c); EDPB para. 117, p. 39
Storage periodHow long, or the start and length of the period; not just a reference to statutory periods15(1)(d); EDPB para. 118, p. 39
RightsThe rights that apply to this processing, and the right to complain to a regulator15(1)(e) and (f); EDPB para. 119, pp. 39 to 40
Source"any available information as to their source", if not collected from the person15(1)(g); EDPB para. 120, p. 40
Automated decisionsWhether they exist; if so, meaningful information about the logic, the significance and the consequences15(1)(h); EDPB para. 121, p. 40
TransfersThe safeguards for transfers outside the EUArticle 15(2)

Other points from the EDPB: give the data as it stood when the request arrived, even if inaccurate or unlawfully processed, and correct or delete it afterwards: "It has to give access first and delete the data afterwards" (Example 6, p. 19). Say to which point in time the answer refers (text after Example 6, p. 19). Send it securely, for example encrypted or password-protected (para. 40, p. 19). Remove only what would adversely affect others (Article 15(4); p. 5).

02

Confirming other action

After rectification, erasure or restriction, the organisation tells each recipient of the data, unless impossible or disproportionate, and tells the person who the recipients are if they ask (Article 19). After erasure of data it made public, it takes reasonable steps to inform other controllers (Article 17(2)). It tells the person before lifting a restriction (Article 18(3)). After an objection to direct marketing, the data "shall no longer be processed for such purposes" (Article 21(3)). For portability, the data is given "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format" (Article 20(1)).

03

Letter A: answer to an access request

Attach the copy and the annex. Delete what does not apply: the password sentence if the copy goes by post, the paragraph on removed parts if nothing was withheld. For the format, write for example: a PDF file and a CSV export.

04

Letter B: confirmation of action taken

Keep the paragraph for the right that was used. Delete the others. Delete the sentence on data kept if everything was erased, and the sentence on recipients not informed if all were informed.

How the organisation handles it: Handling data requests: procedure and register. Its replies: confirm receipt, confirm identity, extend the time limit, refuse or charge a fee, the answer.

The letters from the person: access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, automated decisions.
