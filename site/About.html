01

Who made it

I’m a law student: a master’s degree in law from Italy, an LLM under way in England, and a Certified Information Privacy Professional/Europe (CIPP/E) since June 2025 (IAPP).

Regulators’ decisions are public but scattered across dozens of websites in many languages. Fino puts them in one place.

A personal project: free, no ads, no sign-up, not a law firm. I’m not a lawyer yet, and nothing here is legal advice.

Built with Claude Code, an AI coding assistant. The originals are read with AI help; every fact taken from them comes with the decision’s exact words, which a program checks one by one. The explainers are checked quote by quote against the official text.

02

How it works

Every week a program reads the CMS Enforcement Tracker and GDPRhub, merges every decision into one database (SQLite), cleans it and publishes it here, crediting both. Every run is logged.

Then Fino goes to the source: it reads the regulator’s own document, checks the facts against it (the amount, the date, who was fined, whether a court changed it) and writes its own summary, crediting the regulator. 1,273 of the 5,058 decisions have a summary Fino wrote this way (593 read in part). Each page says what was read. The number grows every week.

03

Where the data comes from

  • The CMS Enforcement Tracker: fines published by European data protection authorities.
  • GDPRhub, run by noyb: decisions with and without fines, with short English summaries by its volunteers.
  • EUR-Lex for the GDPR, consolidated with the published corrections. Only the Official Journal version is legally binding.

Both data sources are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so Fino uses the same licence. Nearly every decision page links to the regulator’s original and to its source.

What Fino holds

  • Italy: 783 decisions, 594 read from an official document, in full or in part (214 in part). Latest: 20 Oct 2026.
  • Germany: 286 decisions, 67 read (48 in part). Latest: 24 Sep 2026.
  • Other countries and EU bodies: 3,989 decisions, 612 read (331 in part). 1,799 carry the collection’s summary, 639 a summary Fino wrote, 1,551 none yet.

Each decision page says where its summary comes from:

  • Fino’s own summary, written from an official document: a decision or part of it, a court ruling, a press release, an annual report or one line of a list of sanctions. Every fact is checked word for word against it.
  • A public collection’s summary (CMS Enforcement Tracker or GDPRhub), as recorded. The original is not read yet.
  • Fino’s summary from a news report or a law firm’s article (9 decisions).
  • None yet: the page links to the source.

Also: 28 laws, 53 Court of Justice rulings, 27 EDPB and WP29 documents and 17 documents of Fino’s own.

04

The rules I follow

  • Nothing is guessed. A date is only as precise as the source gives it: a day, a month or a year. When the source has none and I find one, I record the month and year, never an invented day.
  • One decision, shown once. A decision in both sources is shown once; both records are kept.
  • Who it is against. A company is named only when the source makes clear the decision is about it. Unclear roles show as “named in the source, roles not yet checked”; when no one is named, the page says so or uses the source’s words (“Hospital”).
  • Private people are never named. They are described (“Doctor”) and marked as a private person.
  • Other currencies keep their own amount as the real figure. For comparison they are converted to euro at the European Central Bank rate of the decision day (or the month or year average, when that is all the source gives), marked ≈ and counted in the totals.
  • Same company, same group. Linked when the legal name matches; a name without a legal form (S.A., GmbH) only within one country. Groups (Meta, Google and others) are checked by hand: an EU headquarters and its US parent stay separate companies in one group.
  • Case numbers like 2026/IT/108: year, country, number (ND when the date is unknown). Never reused; a decision that later gets its date gets a new number, and the old link still leads to it.
  • Checked before it changes. Unclear parties, possible duplicates and amounts that do not add up are checked against the original, and a second, independent reading must agree. Until then the site shows what the source says and marks what is uncertain. What is still unclear, I decide myself.
05

What it gets wrong

  • Some duplicates still slip through, especially between decisions without a fine.
  • The topics (“Data security”, “Consent”…) come from simple rules, not from reading each decision. Use them as filters, not conclusions.
  • Most decisions still rest on the two sources, and not every one has a summary yet. Missing decisions are being added from the regulators’ own lists.
  • A fine a court annulled or reduced is marked when I know about it, but not every appeal is known yet.

Corrections made since 5 November 2026 are in a small data file.

Reuse and cite
