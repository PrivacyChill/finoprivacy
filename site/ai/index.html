Short answer: Yes in both countries. I’m confident of that conclusion. I couldn’t read the Garante and German list PDFs, though, so the exact item numbers and wording below are not verified. Check them against the linked documents before you cite them.

What I couldn’t verify

Italy

  • Document: Garante, provvedimento n. 467 of 11 Oct 2018, Allegato 1 (doc. web n. 9058979). It was published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 269 of 19 Nov 2018.
    • Allegato 1, official text
    • Copy hosted by the EDPB
  • Applicable item (from memory): item 1, which covers scoring or evaluative processing on a large scale, including profiling and predictive activities.
    • A second candidate is item 2, on automated processing that produces legal or similarly significant effects. That fits if the tool’s score decides who gets screened out.
    • Confirm the numbering and wording against the PDF.
  • Caveat on "large scale": item 1 is limited to large-scale processing. A retailer with high-volume or seasonal hiring will probably meet it, but record why.

Germany

  • Document: the DSK (joint conference of the German authorities) "Liste von Verarbeitungsvorgängen nach Art. 35 Abs. 4 DS-GVO" for the non-public sector, version 1.1 of 17 Oct 2018. The Land authorities and the BfDI republish it.
    • Niedersachsen overview page
    • PDF on the Niedersachsen site
    • Hamburg copy
    • BfDI overview
  • Applicable item (from memory): the entry on using AI to process personal data in order to evaluate personal aspects of data subjects or steer interaction with them. I believe it is item 2, but I haven’t confirmed that. I found no separate applicant-specific entry.
  • Competent authority: the authority of the Land where each German establishment is located. The DSK list is common to all of them, so the entry is the same everywhere.

EDPB guidance

  • WP248 rev.01, DPIA guidelines (Art. 29 Working Party, 4 Oct 2017). The EDPB endorsed it on 25 May 2018.
  • Why it applies: the tool meets at least these criteria:
    • evaluation or scoring and profiling;
    • possibly automated decision-making with significant effect;
    • innovative technology;
    • arguably vulnerable data subjects. Applicants are in an imbalanced position, which the guidelines discuss for employees.
  • The guidelines say that meeting two or more criteria usually means a DPIA is required.
  • Legal basis: Art. 35(1) and 35(3)(a) GDPR apply independently of the national lists. Art. 35(3)(a) covers systematic and extensive evaluation based on automated processing, including profiling, on which decisions with significant effects are based. The national lists supplement it.

Other points

  • Under the EU AI Act, AI used to screen or evaluate job applicants is high-risk (Annex III, point 4). That brings separate obligations, so check the current application dates. I didn’t verify the AI Act text in this session.
  • If the tool makes decisions solely by automated means, Art. 22 GDPR applies. In Germany, works council co-determination may also apply.

Short answer

I can’t confirm "mandatory" for either country without facts about the tool. What Fino holds is below. Everything in the "not from Fino" paragraph is my own reading.

  • Italy: the closest Garante list entries are item 1 (scoring/profiling) and item 2 (automated decisions with significant effects). Item 1 has a large-scale condition, and item 2 depends on whether decisions are automated.
  • Germany: there is no DSK list entry that names CV scoring as such. The nearest is DSK item 11 (AI used to assess personal aspects of people). Item 5 (combining data from several sources, with opaque algorithms, for decisions with significant effects) may also fit.
  • EU level: Art. 35(1), Art. 35(3)(a) and WP 248 rev.01 apply in both countries.

Show the whole answer

The law

GDPR Art. 35 on Fino (Read in the original, EUR-Lex consolidated text):

  • Art. 35(1): "Where a type of processing in particular using new technologies, and taking into account the nature, scope, context and purposes of the processing, is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller shall, prior to the processing, carry out an assessment…"
  • Art. 35(3)(a): a DPIA is required for "a systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects relating to natural persons which is based on automated processing, including profiling, and on which decisions are based that produce legal effects concerning the natural person or similarly significantly affect the natural person".

Italy: Garante list

The list is Allegato 1 to provvedimento n. 467 of 11 October 2018 (doc. web n. 9059358). Fino holds it as 12 items. See the Fino DPIA checklist.

  • Item 1: evaluation or scoring of people on a large scale, or profiling or prediction about aspects such as work performance, reliability or behaviour. Fino notes this is its own reading of the Italian sentence. It reads "su larga scala" as qualifying only the scoring, and treats profiling and prediction as alternatives. A DPO should confirm that reading.
  • Item 2: automated processing to take decisions with legal or similarly significant effects, including decisions that stop a person exercising a right or using a good or service. Whether this fits depends on whether the tool’s output is a decision or only an input to a recruiter.
  • Item 7: innovative technologies, including AI. It applies only when at least one other WP 248 criterion also applies, so it would likely be met together with scoring.
  • WP 248 two-criteria rule: Fino says the Garante treats two or more WP 248 criteria as requiring a DPIA (provv. 467/2018, premesse). WP 248 itself says "in most cases".

Fino does not test the scope the Garante gives its list, which is processing subject to the consistency mechanism. That is a known gap.

Germany: DSK list

The list is the DSK Muss-Liste for non-public bodies, version 1.1 (17.10.2018). Fino holds 17 items. A retailer is a non-public body, subject to the sector-scope check in § 2 BDSG.

  • Item 11: AI used to steer interaction with people or to assess personal aspects of them. The DSK’s examples are customer-support AI and call-centre mood analysis, not recruitment.
  • Item 5: combining personal data from different sources on a large scale, where not all data come from the person, using algorithms people cannot follow, as a basis for decisions with legal or similar effects. This needs facts about your tool’s data sources and transparency.
  • Not covered: Fino has not checked whether a Land authority adds items to the DSK list, and it holds only version 1.1. Check your competent Land authority’s list and whether a newer DSK version exists.
  • Related: under BDSG § 38, a controller whose processing requires a DPIA must designate a DPO regardless of headcount. Check the current text at the link.

Court rulings and guidance

WP 248 rev.01 on Fino (Article 29 Working Party, endorsed by the EDPB on 25 May 2018, Endorsement 1/2018; Read in the original, whole document):

  • Section III.B: "In most cases, a data controller can consider that a processing meeting two criteria would require a DPIA to be carried out."
  • Section III.B, after criterion 9: "the more criteria are met by the processing, the more likely it is to present a high risk… and therefore to require a DPIA".
  • Criterion 9: "processing operations that aims at allowing, modifying or refusing data subjects' access to a service or entry into a contract". Fino’s extract does not say whether this covers employment. Check the original.

Not in Fino’s data: the Fino extracts do not include WP 248’s evaluation/scoring, innovative-technology or vulnerable-data-subjects criteria. Read the original text for those. Fino holds no newer EDPB guidance specific to DPIAs. Fino holds 27 EDPB/WP29 documents in total.

Fino has an explainer on this topic: "A recruiter makes the final call, so our CV screening is not automated." Is that enough? I have not read it.

Decisions

I did not search Fino’s decisions for CV-screening cases. Fino links 48 Italian and 2 German decisions to Art. 35. One example is the Garante’s decision against Foodinho (2021/IT/043), which concerned algorithmic management of riders and a DPIA breach (Read in the original, in part; appeal status: final). It is about rider management, not recruitment.

Not in Fino’s data

  • Whether your tool meets the "large scale" or "automated decision" conditions.
  • The Land supplements and the later versions of the DSK list.
  • The AI Act classification. AI Act Art. 27 and Art. 26 refer to DPIAs, and I have not run ai_act_check. (Not from Fino: my understanding is that AI used for recruitment is listed as high-risk in AI Act Annex III. Verify this.)

Sources and notice

Not from Fino, my own view: if the tool scores applicants and the score drives or heavily influences who is rejected, a DPIA is the safe course in both countries. The Italian list items 1 and 2, the German item 11, and Art. 35(3)(a) all point that way. Calling it "mandatory" depends on the facts above, and a DPO should decide it.

In which three cases does Art. 35(3) GDPR make a DPIA mandatory?

In which three cases does Art. 35(3) GDPR make a DPIA mandatory?

  • Fino get_law_article

Short answer

Art. 35(3) GDPR names three cases in which a DPIA is required:

  1. Automated evaluation and profiling: a systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects based on automated processing, including profiling, on which decisions with legal or similarly significant effects are based.
  2. Large-scale sensitive data: large-scale processing of special categories of data (Art. 9(1)) or of data on criminal convictions and offences (Art. 10).
  3. Public-area monitoring: systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale.
Show the whole answer

The law

Read in the original (consolidated text on EUR-Lex). The facts come from Fino (finoprivacy.com), GDPR Art. 35 on Fino.

Art. 35(3) opens: "A data protection impact assessment referred to in paragraph 1 shall in particular be required in the case of:"

  • (a) "a systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects relating to natural persons which is based on automated processing, including profiling, and on which decisions are based that produce legal effects concerning the natural person or similarly significantly affect the natural person;"
  • (b) "processing on a large scale of special categories of data referred to in Article 9(1), or of personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences referred to in Article 10; or"
  • (c) "a systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale."

The words "in particular" matter. The three cases are examples of when the Art. 35(1) high-risk test is met, and they are not a closed list. Art. 35(1) itself requires a DPIA where processing is "likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons". Under Art. 35(4), each supervisory authority also publishes a list of processing that needs a DPIA.

Related articles: Art. 9, Art. 10, Art. 22.

FAQ

What can I ask?
  • Decisions of every country: “Which Italian and German decisions are about employee email monitoring?”
  • Laws: “What does Article 35(3) of the GDPR say?”
  • Court of Justice rulings: “What did the Court decide in Schrems II?”
  • EDPB guidance: “What do the EDPB’s consent guidelines say about a pre-ticked box?”
  • Documents and checklists: “Which Fino letters fit a request for a copy of someone’s data?”
  • The tools: “Check facial recognition at a staff entrance in Germany against the DPIA lists.”

Fino holds decisions of 33 countries and the EU’s own bodies, not every decision. Ask “What does Fino hold for Spain, and what are the gaps?”

Does Fino keep my questions?

No. Fino’s program writes no search text to any log. The host, Google Cloud Run in Frankfurt, keeps a request log (the calling address, the calling program, the time and other technical details) for 30 days, then deletes it. Google stores that log, not necessarily in Frankfurt. What you type into your AI is under that company’s terms: check its training setting. Fino needs no personal data or client documents, so do not type them in. Details: Privacy, section 04.

Is it free? Which plan do I need?

Yes. For Claude, Anthropic says custom connectors are on the Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise plans, and Free is limited to one custom connector. On Team and Enterprise only certain administrators can add it, so ask yours.

Is it legal advice?

No. Fino shows what regulators and courts decided and what the law says. A draft it helps with is for a professional to check.

Can the AI get it wrong?

Yes. An AI writes the answer and can misread a fact or leave one out. Open the Fino page behind each answer and check it against the original. Start with “Using only Fino:” to keep the AI to Fino’s data.

Why “Allow always”?

All 26 Fino tools only read Fino’s public data: they change nothing and pass nothing on. Claude sends them only short search terms (a few words, a code or a date), which Fino does not keep. If you prefer to check each call, do not choose Allow always.

Can I use ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot?

Not yet. They are coming soon, and Fino has not tested them.

Something looks wrong. What do I do?

Open the Fino page the answer links to. If it is wrong, write to hello@finoprivacy.com with the decision number. Fino keeps a public list of corrections (Corrections).
