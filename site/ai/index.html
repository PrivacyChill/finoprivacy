What was the Garante’s fine on Enel Energia for telemarketing in 2024, and is it final?

What was the Garante’s fine on Enel Energia for telemarketing in 2024, and is it final? Use ONLY the fino tools; give the Fino links.

  • Fino search_decisions
  • Fino get_decision
  • Fino search_decisions

The fine: €79,107,101, imposed on Enel Energia SpA by the Garante in its decision of 8 February 2024 (Provvedimento n. 81). The Garante announced it in a press release on 29 February 2024.

Fino’s summary says the fine covers serious shortcomings in how personal data was processed for telemarketing. Enel Energia had acquired 978 contracts from companies outside its sales network. Its customer-management and activation systems also had security weaknesses that unauthorised agents exploited for years, and at least 9,300 contracts were activated through these violations. The decision cites GDPR Arts. 5(1)(f), 5(2), 24(1), 25, 28 and 32.

Show the whole answer

Is it final? Not yet. Fino records two stages:

  • Confirmed in court: the Rome Tribunal rejected Enel’s challenge and confirmed the decision in full (judgment 12783/2025, published 18 September 2025). The fine is unchanged at €79,107,101.
  • Further appeal pending: Fino notes that another appeal is still pending. The fine therefore isn’t final, and Fino’s data doesn’t say what that appeal is or when it will be decided.

Fino’s data has no date for the confirmation step, though the judgment’s publication date is given above.

Other Enel Energia decisions: Fino holds two more.

  • A 2026 fine of €563,052 (decision date 12 March 2026) for unwanted promotional calls to a customer on the opposition register. Fino has no court status for it.
  • A 2021 fine of €26,513,977 (decision date 16 December 2021), which Fino lists as appealed. Fino’s note: “Back in court: the Court of Cassation (judgment 18583/2025) quashed the Rome Tribunal judgment and sent the case back for a new hearing, which is pending.”

What did the Garante fine Replika (Luka Inc.) in 2025 and why?

What did the Garante fine Replika (Luka Inc.) in 2025 and why? Use ONLY the fino tools; give the Fino links.

  • Fino search_decisions
  • Fino get_decision

The Garante fined Luka Inc., the provider of the “virtual companion” Replika AI, €5,000,000 on 10 April 2025. Fino lists the decision as 2025/IT/077.

Why, according to Fino’s summary:

  • It processed personal data unlawfully.
  • It breached the transparency rules.
  • It failed to put effective age verification in place.
Show the whole answer

GDPR articles cited:

  • Art. 3(2)(a), territorial scope
  • Art. 5(1)(a), lawfulness, fairness and transparency
  • Art. 5(1)(c), data minimisation
  • Art. 6, lawfulness of processing
  • Art. 12 and Art. 13, transparent information to data subjects
  • Art. 24, controller responsibility
  • Art. 25(1), data protection by design

Fino classes the violation as “non-compliance with general data processing principles”. The topics it tags are accountability, data principles, transparency and unlawful processing.

Status: the fine is under appeal. Fino notes a court challenge is pending, so the Garante has not published the order as a penalty. The fine amount is still shown as €5,000,000, and Fino has no appeal date.

Limits of Fino’s data: the record gives no regulator reference number and no quotes from the decision. The reasoning above is only the one-sentence summary, which Fino credits to GDPRhub. Check anything finer against the original.

In which three cases does Art. 35(3) GDPR make a DPIA mandatory?

In which three cases does Art. 35(3) GDPR make a DPIA mandatory? Use ONLY the fino tools; give the Fino links.

  • Fino get_law_article
  • Fino reference

Article 35(3) GDPR requires a DPIA in three cases. The wording below is Fino’s plain-language summary from its Art. 35 page and its “Do you need a DPIA?” checklist. It is not the statutory text.

  1. Art. 35(3)(a): You systematically and extensively evaluate people by automated means, including profiling, and make decisions that affect them legally or in a similarly significant way.
  2. Art. 35(3)(b): You process special categories of data, or criminal-offence data, on a large scale.
  3. Art. 35(3)(c): You systematically monitor a publicly accessible area on a large scale, for example CCTV of a street.

FAQ

What can I ask?
  • Decisions of every country: “Which Italian and German decisions are about employee email monitoring?”
  • Laws: “What does Article 35(3) of the GDPR say?”
  • Court of Justice rulings: “What did the Court decide in Schrems II?”
  • EDPB guidance: “What do the EDPB’s consent guidelines say about a pre-ticked box?”
  • Documents and checklists: “Which Fino letters fit a request for a copy of someone’s data?”
  • The tools: “Check facial recognition at a staff entrance in Germany against the DPIA lists.”

Fino holds decisions of 33 countries and the EU’s own bodies, not every decision. Ask “What does Fino hold for Spain, and what are the gaps?”

Does Fino keep my questions?

No. Fino’s program writes no search text to any log. The host, Google Cloud Run in Frankfurt, keeps a request log (the calling address, the calling program, the time and other technical details) for 30 days, then deletes it. Google stores that log, not necessarily in Frankfurt. What you type into your AI is under that company’s terms: check its training setting. Fino needs no personal data or client documents, so do not type them in. Details: Privacy, section 04.

Is it free? Which plan do I need?

Yes. For Claude, Anthropic says custom connectors are on the Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise plans, and Free is limited to one custom connector. On Team and Enterprise only certain administrators can add it, so ask yours.

Is it legal advice?

No. Fino shows what regulators and courts decided and what the law says. A draft it helps with is for a professional to check.

Can the AI get it wrong?

Yes. An AI writes the answer and can misread a fact or leave one out. Open the Fino page behind each answer and check it against the original. Start with “Using only Fino:” to keep the AI to Fino’s data.

Why “Allow always”?

All 26 Fino tools only read Fino’s public data: they change nothing and pass nothing on. Claude sends them only short search terms (a few words, a code or a date), which Fino does not keep. If you prefer to check each call, do not choose Allow always.

Can I use ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot?

Not yet. They are coming soon, and Fino has not tested them.

Something looks wrong. What do I do?

Open the Fino page the answer links to. If it is wrong, write to hello@finoprivacy.com with the decision number. Fino keeps a public list of corrections (About).
