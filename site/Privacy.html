Privacy · last changed 9 November 2026

Privacy. I collect nothing about visitors.

I don’t collect, store or see anything about you as a visitor. This site uses no cookies, analytics, trackers or ads. Nothing you type into these pages is sent anywhere, and they don’t load anything from other websites. Even the fonts are served from this site. The connector for Claude is a separate service: see below.

  • Visitors: I collect nothing.
  • The connector (for Claude): Google’s hosting records who calls it; Fino keeps no search text. See below.
  • If you email me: I keep your email only to reply, and delete it 12 months after our last message.
  • People in the decisions: Fino never publishes anything that identifies them; see below.
01

Who is responsible

Fino is run by Lorenzo A., a private individual in the United Kingdom. I decide how the personal data Fino holds is used, so I am its “controller”. You can reach me at hello@finoprivacy.com. Fino has no data protection officer, as it isn’t required to appoint one (Article 37 GDPR).

Fino is run from the UK, so the UK GDPR applies. Where the EU GDPR applies too, I follow it as well. The article numbers on this page are the same in both.

02

What the host records

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub (a US company, part of Microsoft) uses your IP address to deliver the pages and, as its documentation says, logs it for security. That falls under GitHub’s privacy statement. GitHub doesn’t say how long it keeps these logs. I don’t receive them and I don’t see who visits.

03

What stays in your browser

  • Searching and filtering happen in your browser. What you type isn’t sent anywhere, and nor are your answers in the tools.
  • If you press the light/dark button, your browser remembers that choice on your device.
  • If you press the look button (Corporate or Fino look), your browser remembers that choice on your device.
  • If you tick points in the checklists, your browser keeps the ticks on your device so they’re still there next time.
  • The calendar file in the response timeline is made in your browser, and the copy buttons only put text on your clipboard when you press them.

None of this is ever sent to me or anyone else. You can clear it at any time in your browser settings.

04

The connector

Fino also has a connector that lets Claude look things up in Fino (see Fino with your AI). It is a separate program from these pages, and it runs on Google Cloud Run in Frankfurt (the region europe-west3). It reads the same public data as these pages: the decisions of every country Fino holds, the laws, the Court of Justice rulings, the EDPB guidance and Fino’s documents. Its tools only read: start_here, search_decisions, get_decision, get_law_article, dpia_check, coverage, corrections, search_rulings, get_ruling, search_guidance, get_guidance, compare_decisions, slide_table, statistics, drafting_guide, search_documents, get_document, similar_decisions, search_laws, reference, search, fetch, response_timeline, which_regulator, cookie_check and ai_act_check.

  • What the host records. Each call to the connector reaches Google Cloud Run, whose request log records the address the call came from, the name of the program that called (the “user agent”), the time, and technical details of the request (the address requested, the status, the size and the time taken). The calling address is that of the AI service that makes the call: for Claude, Anthropic’s servers, not your own. A program that calls the connector directly shows its own address. Google keeps this log for 30 days, then deletes it. The log is stored by Google, not necessarily in Frankfurt (Google is certified under the Data Privacy Framework, see section 05).
  • What Fino’s program keeps. No search text. Searches are short (a keyword of up to 40 characters, or a question of up to 200) and are answered from Fino’s public data. It does not write the text of a search, or an address, to any file or log.
  • What it counts. In memory only, so a restart erases it, and the connector as it runs today does not print or send the counts anywhere: how many times each tool was called, how many calls found something, how many found nothing and how many were refused because the input was not valid, with the country codes, article numbers or terms from Fino’s own vocabulary involved. Never the text of what was asked. To stop floods, it also counts calls per address, in memory only.
  • What you type into Claude. That is governed by Anthropic’s terms and privacy policy, not by Fino’s. Fino receives only what Claude sends it.

There is no login and no upload, and the connector holds no personal data about you beyond that short-lived count per address. My reason for the counts, and for using a host that keeps a request log, is my legitimate interest in keeping the connector working. The counts stop floods; they could also show which questions it cannot answer yet, but nothing prints them today, and this page will change before that does (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Your rights are in section 07.

05

If you email Fino

I use your address and your message only to reply to you. My reason is my legitimate interest in answering you (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Emailing is up to you; I just need your address to answer.

Mail to hello@finoprivacy.com is passed on by Cloudflare (which runs the domain) to a Gmail inbox, so Cloudflare and Google process your email. Both are US companies certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension. The EU and the UK have both formally decided that this framework protects personal data well enough (an “adequacy decision”).

I delete emails 12 months after our last message, unless I need one for longer to deal with a legal claim.

06

People in the decisions

Fino lists decisions published by data protection regulators and courts, read directly or through two public databases of decisions, the CMS Enforcement Tracker and GDPRhub.

Fino never publishes anything that identifies private individuals in the decisions. Their names are left out, even when a source gives them, and they are described only by their role, for example “a doctor”. Companies and public bodies are named as the sources name them. The GDPR protects people, not legal persons, so a company’s name, legal form and contact details are not personal data (Article 4(1) and Recital 14 GDPR). Sole traders are treated as private individuals and are not named. If you think you can still be identified from a page, write to me and I’ll fix it.

To check decisions against the originals, I use several AI tools: Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google), OpenAI’s Codex and Moonshot AI’s Kimi. Only the decisions go to them, as the regulators published them; some include the names and roles of people. Nothing about visitors or people who email me goes to any of them. For Claude I have switched off the setting that lets Anthropic train on them; I have not checked that setting for the others. Anthropic, Google and OpenAI are US companies and Moonshot AI is based in China; their own terms say where they process the data and how long they keep it.

To check the decisions, I keep private working copies of them as the sources published them, on my computer and, when a cloud session reads them, on Anthropic’s servers. They can include the names and roles of the people involved and what the regulator found about them. The notes the AI makes from them, with word-for-word extracts from the decisions, are kept in a private backup with GitHub, which is in the US and certified under the same framework as above. That framework covers GitHub only; it says nothing about the AI tools named above. A copy of the backup is on Anthropic’s servers when I work with Claude in the cloud. None of this is ever published. Copies are kept while the decision is on Fino. There is no automatic deletion yet. The backup keeps earlier versions of the notes, with no end date. I do all this to inform the public about how data protection law is enforced, which is my legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

07

Your rights

If you have emailed me, used the connector, or are in one of the decisions, you can ask me for a copy of your data, or ask me to correct it, delete it or limit how I use it. It’s free. I answer within one month, and I try to answer within a week. Fino doesn’t rely on your consent or on a contract with you, so the right to take your data elsewhere doesn’t apply, and there is no consent to withdraw.

Your right to object. You can object at any time to my use of your data, for reasons to do with your particular situation. I will then stop, unless I can show compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or I need the data for a legal claim (Article 21 GDPR).

Nothing about you is decided automatically, and there is no profiling.

08

Search engines and AI assistants

The pages are public, so search engines and AI assistants may read them and show them in their results. That’s on purpose: the point of Fino is to make the decisions easy to find. If you reuse or quote Fino, whether you’re a person or an AI, please credit it with a link to finoprivacy.com.

09

Complaints

You can always complain to a data protection authority: the ICO in the UK, or in the EU the authority where you live or work, or where you think the problem happened. Which regulator? helps you find it.

Questions about your data: hello@finoprivacy.com
