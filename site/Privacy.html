Privacy · last changed 13 October 2026

Privacy. I collect nothing about visitors.

I don’t collect, store or see anything about you as a visitor. This site uses no cookies, analytics, trackers or ads. Nothing you type into it is sent anywhere, and it doesn’t load anything from other websites. Even the fonts are served from this site.

  • Cookies
  • Analytics
  • Trackers
  • Ads
  • Outside fonts or scripts
  • Visitors: I collect nothing.
  • If you email me: I keep your email only to reply, and delete it 12 months after our last message.
  • People in the decisions: Fino never publishes anything that identifies them; see below.

Who is responsible

Fino is run by Lorenzo A., a private individual in the United Kingdom. I decide how the personal data Fino holds is used, so I am its “controller”. You can reach me at hello@finoprivacy.com. Fino has no data protection officer, as it isn’t required to appoint one (Article 37 GDPR).

Fino is run from the UK, so the UK GDPR applies. Where the EU GDPR applies too, I follow it as well. The article numbers on this page are the same in both.

What the host records

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub (a US company, part of Microsoft) uses your IP address to deliver the pages and, as its documentation says, logs it for security. That falls under GitHub’s privacy statement. GitHub doesn’t say how long it keeps these logs. I don’t receive them and I don’t see who visits.

What stays in your browser

  • Searching and filtering happen in your browser. What you type isn’t sent anywhere, and nor are your answers in the tools.
  • If you press the light/dark button, your browser remembers that choice on your device.
  • If you tick points in the checklists, your browser keeps the ticks on your device so they’re still there next time.
  • The calendar file in the response timeline is made in your browser, and the copy buttons only put text on your clipboard when you press them.

None of this is ever sent to me or anyone else. You can clear it at any time in your browser settings.

If you email Fino

I use your address and your message only to reply to you. My reason is my legitimate interest in answering you (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Emailing is up to you; I just need your address to answer.

Mail to hello@finoprivacy.com is passed on by Cloudflare (which runs the domain) to a Gmail inbox, so Cloudflare and Google process your email. Both are US companies certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension. The EU and the UK have both formally decided that this framework protects personal data well enough (an “adequacy decision”).

I delete emails 12 months after our last message, unless I need one for longer to deal with a legal claim.

People in the decisions

Fino lists decisions published by data protection regulators and courts, read directly or through two public databases of decisions, the CMS Enforcement Tracker and GDPRhub.

Fino never publishes anything that identifies private individuals in the decisions. Their names are left out, even when a source gives them, and they are described only by their role, for example “a doctor”. Companies and public bodies are named as the sources name them; information about them isnisn’t personal data (Recital 14 GDPR)rsquo;t personal data, which is only about people (Article 4(1) and Recital 14 GDPR). If you think you can still be identified from a page, write to me and I’ll fix it.

To check decisions against the originals, I use Claude, an AI tool made by Anthropic. Only the decisions go to Claude, as the regulators published them, and I have switched off the setting that lets Anthropic use them to train its models. Nothing about visitors or people who email me goes to Anthropic.

To check the decisions, I keep private working copies of them as the sources published them. They can include the names and roles of the people involved and what the regulator found about them. They are kept on my computer and in a private backup with GitHub (in the US, certified under the same framework as above), are never published, and are kept only while the decision is on Fino. I do all this to inform the public about how data protection law is enforced, which is my legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Your rights

If you have emailed me, or you are in one of the decisions, you can ask me for a copy of your data, or ask me to correct it, delete it or limit how I use it. It’s free. I answer within one month, and I try to answer within a week. Fino doesn’t rely on your consent or on a contract with you, so the right to take your data elsewhere doesn’t apply, and there is no consent to withdraw.

Your right to object. You can object at any time to my use of your data, for reasons to do with your particular situation. I will then stop, unless I can show compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or I need the data for a legal claim (Article 21 GDPR).

Nothing about you is decided automatically, and there is no profiling.

Search engines and AI assistants

The pages are public, so search engines and AI assistants may read them and show them in their results. That’s on purpose: the point of Fino is to make the decisions easy to find. If you reuse or quote Fino, whether you’re a person or an AI, please credit it with a link to finoprivacy.com.

Complaints

You can always complain to a data protection authority: the ICO in the UK, or in the EU the authority where you live or work, or where you think the problem happened. Which regulator? helps you find it.
